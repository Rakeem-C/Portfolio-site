Every time AI capability takes a step forward, the gains don’t spread evenly. They concentrate.

The people closest to the center, the researchers and engineers at frontier labs and the technical teams around them, are becoming *orders of magnitude* more productive. They know which model to use for which job, how to structure a task so an agent can finish it, and how to check the output. For them, each new release multiplies what they could already do.

Move away from the center and the gain shrinks fast. Most people use AI the way they used a search engine in 2003: occasionally, shallowly and with low trust. For the owner of a twelve-person HVAC company, the gain so far rounds to zero.

That’s a power law. A small fraction of people captures most of the value, and the curve gets steeper with every jump in capability.

## Access isn’t the root cause

It’s tempting to call this an access problem. It isn’t. Anyone can open a chat window today. Look at it from every angle and the real constraints show up underneath:

- **Knowing what’s possible.** You can’t ask for what you don’t know exists. People at the frontier see capability first-hand, months before it’s common knowledge.
- **Knowing how to structure work.** Models do well on well-specified tasks. Turning messy, real-world work into well-specified tasks is a skill, and it’s unevenly distributed.
- **Knowing how to verify.** If you can’t tell a good output from a confident wrong one, you can’t trust AI with anything that matters.
- **Fit to the workflow.** A tool in a separate tab gets used once in a while. A tool built into how the work already happens gets used every day.

None of these are fixed by a cheaper subscription.

## Bending the median

The goal isn’t to make the top 1% more productive. They’re doing fine. The goal is to bend the median: to change the shape of the curve so the typical business, and the typical worker, captures a real share of the gain.

That means carrying what the frontier knows (what works, what breaks, how to check it) into places that will never hire an AI researcher, and building it into the work itself.
